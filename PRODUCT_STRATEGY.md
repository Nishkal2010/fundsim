# FundSim Product Strategy

## fundsimulate.com — Finance Simulator for PE/VC/IB

**Document Date:** April 2026
**Strategic Horizon:** 18 months

---

## Executive Summary

FundSim occupies a white space that the incumbents — Wall Street Prep, BIWS, and CFI — have systematically ignored: hands-on, interactive deal simulation. Every major competitor sells video instruction plus static Excel templates. None of them let a student actually _run_ a deal. FundSim does.

The core strategic recommendation is to position FundSim as the "practice layer" that sits on top of the instruction layer competitors provide — not a replacement for WSP or BIWS, but the indispensable complement that turns passive knowledge into active competency. This positioning makes FundSim naturally partnership-friendly rather than adversarial with incumbents, while carving out a differentiated and defensible market position.

The path to sustainable revenue runs through university and finance club licensing, not individual subscriptions. Target institutional deals first. Individual monetization follows once institutional adoption creates social proof and brand familiarity at scale.

## 1. Competitive Positioning Analysis

### How the Incumbents Compete

| Competitor                        | Core Model                                            | Price Point                                           | Primary Weakness                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Wall Street Prep                  | Video courses + Excel templates, lifetime access      | $499–$999 per individual; negotiated for universities | Passive consumption, no practice environment         |
| BIWS (Breaking Into Wall Street)  | Video courses + financial model library               | $497/year                                             | Static, no interactivity, no deal simulation         |
| CFI (Corporate Finance Institute) | Subscription-based micro-course library, FMVA cert    | $497–$847/year                                        | Breadth over depth, no simulation                    |
| Wall Street Oasis                 | Community + interview guides + affiliate course sales | $149–$397 (courses)                                   | Community play, not a learning product               |
| AmplifyME                         | Live 2-hour simulations, B2B2University model         | Institutional licensing                               | Markets/trading focused, weak on PE/VC/M&A mechanics |

### The White Space FundSim Owns

The competitors have built a library economy: they sell you knowledge you can consume. None of them have built a practice economy: an environment where you apply that knowledge under realistic deal conditions.

This is not a minor gap. The finance hiring process has shifted heavily toward technical testing — modeling tests, deal case studies, LBO walkthroughs — and no product on the market prepares students to perform under those conditions. FundSim's interactive DCF, LBO, waterfall, and J-curve models are the closest thing the market has to a "finance flight simulator."

### Positioning Statement

**For finance students and early-career professionals who need to demonstrate technical competency, not just claim it, FundSim is the interactive deal simulation platform that builds the muscle memory to perform in live technical interviews and on-the-job modeling tests — because watching videos and reading templates does not prepare you to build under pressure.**

### Positioning Choice: What FundSim Should Be

Among the four options — practice tool, learning tool, interview prep, professional reference — the correct answer is **interview prep + practice tool**, unified under a single concept: **"The Deal Simulator."**

Rationale:

- "Learning tool" puts FundSim in direct competition with WSP and CFI, which have years of brand equity and content depth. This is a losing fight.
- "Professional reference" targets a post-hire audience with different needs and lower urgency to pay.
- "Interview prep" has the highest willingness to pay (students are facing a binary outcome: get the job or don't) and maps directly to FundSim's interactive format.
- "Practice tool" is the mechanism that delivers the interview prep outcome.

The message to students: _"You've watched the videos. Now run the deal."_
The message to universities/clubs: _"We give your students the hands-on reps they need to compete against target school candidates."_

## 2. Monetization Strategy

### Recommended Model: B2B2C Institutional-First Freemium

The optimal monetization architecture has three layers, executed in sequence.

#### Layer 1: Free Core (Current State — Keep It)

Keep the core simulator free forever. This is not charity; it is the acquisition engine. The free tier builds the user base that makes institutional deals credible and creates the social proof loop (see Growth section).

What stays free:

- All current simulator modules (DCF, LBO, waterfall, J-curve, M&A deal mechanics)
- Basic scenario inputs and results
- One saved scenario per model type

#### Layer 2: FundSim Pro — Individual Subscription ($19–$29/month or $149/year)

The freemium paywall should be built around output, collaboration, and depth — not access to the core mechanics.

**Behind the paywall (high perceived value, low development cost):**

| Feature                                                      | Why It Converts                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Downloadable Excel model with full formula mechanics exposed | Students want the Excel file for interviews — this is the single highest-conversion paywall item |
| Unlimited saved scenarios + scenario comparison (multi-case) | Power users run bull/base/bear; free tier limits to one                                          |
| PDF/shareable deal summary report                            | Social proof and interview artifact                                                              |
| Infrastructure finance module                                | Niche but high-demand; early-career infra bankers have no dedicated tool                         |
| Additional asset classes (credit, real assets)               | Expansion depth for serious users                                                                |
| Interview question bank tied to model outputs                | Contextualizes the simulation in actual interview scenarios                                      |

**Pricing rationale:** At $149/year, FundSim is 3x cheaper than the cheapest BIWS plan and requires no video consumption. The value prop is pure utility. Conversion targets: 5–8% of free users within 12 months of launch, consistent with EdTech freemium benchmarks (18–40% trial-to-paid for education apps with high-intent users).

#### Layer 3: Institutional Licensing (Primary Revenue Engine — 6–18 Month Priority)

This is where the real money is. WSP charges universities negotiated rates; some schools pay for bulk access, others get it free in exchange for co-marketing. The model that works for FundSim is different — sell outcomes, not seats.

**University Finance Department Licensing**

- Target: Finance department chairs and curriculum directors at top 50 business schools
- Pitch: "Integrate FundSim as the practical lab component of your Corporate Finance, PE, or M&A courses"
- Pricing model: $3,000–$8,000/year per department (unlimited student access within the department), tiered by student enrollment
- Value delivered: Professors get an interactive assignment and assessment tool; students get structured practice; the department can show employers their students have hands-on deal experience

**Finance Club Licensing**

- Target: Investment banking, PE, and capital markets clubs at top 100 universities
- Pitch: "Give your members the deal simulation reps that put them ahead of candidates from competing schools"
- Pricing model: $500–$1,500/year per club (50–200 members typical), with co-branded deal challenges and leaderboards
- Value delivered: Club leadership can run internal "deal competitions" using FundSim; members get a credential and ranking they can reference in interviews

**Corporate Training Licensing**

- Target: Investment banks, PE funds, and consulting firms for analyst/associate training programs
- Pitch: "Replace the first two weeks of static Excel template review with interactive deal simulations your new hires run before day one"
- Pricing model: $15,000–$60,000/year per firm, priced per seat for firm-wide use or as a cohort tool for new analyst classes
- Note: This is an 18-month+ play; pursue after university channel is validated. WSP already owns this segment at large banks — target mid-market PE funds and boutique banks where WSP's enterprise sales motion has not penetrated.

#### Layer 4: Certification (12+ Month Play)

A "FundSim Deal Certification" — completion certificate for finishing a curated sequence of deal simulations across PE/VC/IB — has credential value if backed by recognizable institutional partners. The certificate itself is not valuable today. It becomes valuable when finance clubs and university programs formally endorse it and when enough employers recognize it.

Do not launch certification without institutional backing. The revenue model is: certificate fee ($49–$99 one-time) + premium subscription required to access the certification track.

## 3. Growth Loops

### Primary Growth Loop: The Interview Artifact Loop

This is FundSim's highest-leverage organic growth mechanism.

1. Student runs a deal simulation on FundSim
2. FundSim generates a shareable deal summary (PDF or public URL) showing their assumptions, outputs, and key metrics
3. Student shares the link in a cover letter, LinkedIn profile, or directly in an interview ("I modeled this LBO last week — here's the output")
4. Interviewer or recruiter visits the FundSim link to view the deal
5. Interviewer discovers FundSim, creates account, potentially shares with their analyst training team
6. The student who shared gets a conversion credit (referral reward: one month of Pro free)

This loop has three properties that make it durable: (1) it requires no paid distribution, (2) it creates a professional artifact that users are intrinsically motivated to share, and (3) the referral lands in front of a high-value audience (finance professionals, not just students).

**Implementation requirement:** Public shareable deal URLs must be built before this loop can operate. This is the single highest-priority growth feature.

### Secondary Growth Loop: The Finance Club Flywheel

1. Partner with one high-profile finance club (target: Wharton Undergraduate Finance Club, Columbia Capital Markets Club, NYU Stern Finance Society)
2. Run a co-branded "Deal Challenge" — a structured LBO or M&A deal competition using FundSim, with a leaderboard
3. Club publishes results and recognizes top performers internally
4. Members share results on LinkedIn ("Ranked #3 at Wharton's FundSim LBO Challenge")
5. Other clubs at peer schools see this, request their own FundSim challenge
6. FundSim converts clubs to paid licensing after demonstrating the value of the competition format

This flywheel works because finance clubs are prestige-driven. Any tool that creates a public ranking signal gives club leadership a reason to adopt it and members a reason to promote it.

**First club acquisition cost:** Do the first 3–5 club partnerships for free. Treat it as a marketing expense. The brand association with Wharton or Columbia is worth more than the $500–$1,500 annual club license fee.

### Tertiary Growth Loop: Professor-to-Cohort Loop

1. Identify professors teaching Corporate Finance, M&A, or PE/VC courses at target schools
2. Offer free faculty access plus a structured "assignment template" (e.g., "Run a 3-scenario LBO on this target company")
3. Professor assigns FundSim as coursework — cohort of 30–80 students creates accounts
4. Students complete assignment, some convert to Pro for downloadable Excel
5. Professor publishes or presents student work, creating FundSim visibility with peer faculty
6. Adjacent professors at the same school or peer schools request access

This is the B2B2C model AmplifyME uses with CFA Institute, adapted for the classroom. AmplifyME's CFA partnership demonstrates that institutional co-branding dramatically accelerates student adoption.

## 4. Feature Prioritization Framework

### Scoring Rationale

Features are evaluated on four dimensions:

- **Student/User Impact (0–10):** Direct improvement to the core user experience
- **Revenue Impact (0–10):** Effect on conversion, retention, or institutional deal-making
- **Effort (0–10, inverse):** 10 = low effort, 1 = high effort
- **Strategic Alignment (0–10):** Fit with positioning as "The Deal Simulator" and growth loops

### Prioritized Feature Backlog

| Rank | Feature                                           | User Impact | Revenue Impact | Effort (inv.) | Strategic Fit | Weighted Score | Build Window          |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------- | ------------- | ------------- | -------------- | --------------------- |
| 1    | Shareable deal summary (public URL + PDF export)  | 8           | 9              | 7             | 10            | **8.6**        | Now (0–6 weeks)       |
| 2    | Excel/model download with mechanics exposed       | 9           | 10             | 6             | 9             | **8.6**        | Now (0–8 weeks)       |
| 3    | Multi-case scenario comparison (bull/base/bear)   | 9           | 7              | 7             | 9             | **8.1**        | Now–Next (6–10 weeks) |
| 4    | Club/cohort leaderboard and deal competition mode | 7           | 9              | 6             | 10            | **8.0**        | Next (2–4 months)     |
| 5    | Infrastructure finance module                     | 7           | 6              | 6             | 8             | **6.9**        | Next (3–5 months)     |
| 6    | Interview question bank tied to model outputs     | 8           | 7              | 7             | 8             | **7.6**        | Next (3–4 months)     |
| 7    | Additional asset classes (credit, real assets)    | 6           | 5              | 4             | 7             | **5.5**        | Later (6–12 months)   |
| 8    | Professor assignment template system              | 5           | 8              | 7             | 9             | **7.3**        | Next (3–5 months)     |

### Why This Order

**Features 1 and 2 (Shareable URLs + Excel Download) are co-equal top priorities.** Both must ship before any monetization infrastructure. The shareable URL is the primary viral growth mechanism. The Excel download is the primary freemium conversion lever. Neither requires a large technical investment relative to the business impact.

**Feature 3 (Multi-case comparison) ships in the same sprint window** because it is the natural upgrade path that makes the Excel download more valuable — a user comparing three scenarios wants to export all three cases, which requires Pro.

**Feature 4 (Leaderboard/Competition Mode) unlocks the club flywheel.** Without a competitive mechanic, club adoption is purely altruistic. A leaderboard gives club leadership a product to organize a competition around.

**Feature 5 (Infrastructure Finance) is a differentiated niche wedge.** No competitor has a dedicated infra modeling simulator. The infra finance community — infrastructure PE, project finance bankers, government-backed fund analysts — is underserved and has strong word-of-mouth dynamics. This creates a second distinct user persona with its own growth loop.

**Feature 6 (Interview Questions) bridges the practice-to-interview-prep positioning.** After a student runs a DCF, serving them the three most common DCF-related interview questions creates a natural content loop and increases session depth.

**Feature 8 (Professor Assignment Templates) enables the professor-to-cohort growth loop.** A professor needs a frictionless way to assign FundSim work. A pre-built assignment template ("Run a 3-scenario LBO on Target Co. X and submit your assumptions and outputs") reduces the adoption barrier from weeks to minutes.

## 5. Partnership Opportunities

### Tier 1: Immediate — Finance Clubs (0–6 Months)

**Strategy:** Offer free club licenses for the first year in exchange for co-branding on club communications and a published post-challenge case study.

**Priority targets:**

| Club                                | School     | Why Them                                                                |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wharton Undergraduate Finance Club  | UPenn      | 2,500+ members, most visible undergrad finance club in the US           |
| Columbia Capital Markets Club       | Columbia   | Strong IB placement, highly selective, prestige multiplier              |
| NYU Stern Finance Society           | NYU        | Large member base, strong BIWS/WSP adoption already (familiar audience) |
| Michigan Ross Investment Club       | U Michigan | Top non-Ivy target school, very active club culture                     |
| UT Austin Finance & Investment Club | UT Austin  | Large state school finance community, growing IB pipeline               |

**Approach:** Email club presidents and VP of Education directly. Offer to run a free "FundSim Deal Challenge" as a club event — provide a structured deal scenario, host the leaderboard, and send completion certificates. Ask for nothing except a LinkedIn post from the club account.

**Avoid:** Do not approach Harvard or Stanford first. Their clubs are heavily recruited and inundated with vendor pitches. Start with clubs that are prestigious but slightly hungrier for differentiated content.

### Tier 2: Near-Term — University Professors (3–9 Months)

**Target profile:** Professors teaching PE/VC, Corporate Finance, M&A, or Financial Modeling at top 50 business schools. Prioritize clinical/practice professors over tenure-track researchers — they have a stronger orientation toward practical tools.

**Pitch:** Free faculty license + a co-authored "FundSim Assignment Guide" they can publish or present at a teaching conference. Professors value content they can attach their name to.

**High-value professor targets:** Look for professors who publish on LinkedIn or tweet about PE/VC/IB education — these are the most likely early adopters with built-in distribution.

### Tier 3: Strategic — Finance Competitions (6–12 Months)

**CFA Research Challenge:** The CFA Institute has already validated the simulator competition model (their 2025 AmplifyME partnership). Approach the CFA Institute's student engagement team with a proposal to integrate FundSim's LBO/DCF simulator as a practice tool for Research Challenge participants. The CFA gets a free resource to direct 10,000+ annual participants toward; FundSim gets institutional validation and mass exposure.

**YIS (Youth in Shaping):** Finance competitions for high school and early college students are an underserved segment. A version of FundSim positioned as a competition preparation tool for DECA, FBLA, or university case competitions creates a feeder pipeline.

**Internal bank competitions:** Several banks run internal analyst modeling competitions (Goldman Sachs, Jefferies, Lazard). Approach training teams at boutique banks — not bulge bracket, where procurement cycles are 12–18 months — to integrate FundSim into new analyst cohort onboarding.

### Tier 4: Evaluated Carefully — BIWS/WSO Partnership

**The opportunity:** WSO's affiliate model generates ~15% of their revenue through commissions. A BIWS or WSO affiliate arrangement — where they promote FundSim Pro as a practice complement to their video courses — could generate meaningful inbound traffic. WSO has 20M+ annual visitors; even a 0.1% conversion on referred traffic is material.

**The risk:** Affiliates align incentives short-term but create dependency and margin compression. If WSO or BIWS builds their own simulation layer (or acquires AmplifyME), the referral tap turns off.

**Recommendation:** Pursue a WSO affiliate listing for brand visibility, but do not rely on it for revenue. Treat it as awareness, not distribution strategy. Negotiate a non-exclusivity clause. Do not offer WSO more than 20% commission.

**Do not pursue a formal partnership with BIWS.** BIWS's competitive positioning (comprehensive modeling library) would make them a natural acquirer of simulation capability. A partnership creates information asymmetry before you are ready for an acquisition conversation.

### Tier 5: Long-Term — Investment Banks for Corporate Training

**The market:** Banks, PE funds, and consulting firms spend significantly on new analyst/associate training. WSP has locked up the bulge bracket. The mid-market is open.

**Target firms:** Boutique and middle-market banks (Houlihan Lokey, Baird, Piper Sandler, William Blair), mid-market PE funds ($500M–$5B AUM where analyst training is informal), and Big 4 transaction advisory groups.

**Pitch:** "Your new analysts have watched WSP videos. Use FundSim to verify they can actually run the models before they sit in front of a client." Position as a training assessment tool, not a replacement for their existing content.

## 6. The 5 Most Impactful Features to Build Next

Listed in strict priority order with rationale:

### 1. Shareable Deal Summary — Public URL + PDF Export

**Why it is #1:** This single feature activates the primary viral growth loop, creates a professional artifact students are intrinsically motivated to share, and requires no paid distribution. Every student who shares a deal analysis link is an unpaid distribution agent. Without this, FundSim relies entirely on search and word-of-mouth — slow and unscalable. With this, every completed deal becomes a growth event.

**Minimum viable version:** A shareable URL that renders a clean, read-only view of the deal: inputs, model outputs, and key metrics. No auth required to view. PDF export can follow in the next iteration.

### 2. Excel Model Download with Full Formula Mechanics

**Why it is #2:** This is the single highest-conversion paywall feature in the market. BIWS and WSP sell Excel model libraries for hundreds of dollars specifically because students want to see the formulas. FundSim can offer a dynamically-generated Excel file based on the user's actual simulation inputs — a personalized model, not a template. This is more valuable than anything BIWS sells because it is built on the student's own scenario.

**Monetization mechanism:** Excel download is a Pro feature. The free-to-paid conversion pitch is simple: "You built the model. Download it and take it to your interview."

### 3. Multi-Case Scenario Comparison (Bull/Base/Bear)

**Why it is #3:** Every IB and PE interview involves sensitivity analysis and scenario comparison. A student who can show a three-case comparison output in an interview is demonstrably more sophisticated than one who shows a single scenario. This feature drives deeper engagement, longer sessions, and a stronger case for the Excel download (three scenarios = one comprehensive deliverable).

**Implementation note:** The comparison view should render side-by-side, not stacked, and should highlight the swing factors between cases (e.g., "Entry multiple drives 40% of IRR variance in bear case").

### 4. Club/Cohort Leaderboard and Competition Mode

**Why it is #4:** This feature unlocks the finance club flywheel, which is the fastest path to institutional adoption. A club president can run a "FundSim LBO Challenge" in a single club meeting. The leaderboard creates a competitive signal students can reference publicly. Without this feature, club adoption is entirely dependent on individual student initiative — too slow. With it, clubs become active distribution partners.

**Implementation note:** The competition mode should support a "sealed bid" format where participants submit their deal assumptions and only see the leaderboard after all submissions are in. This mirrors the real deal dynamics where you don't know competitor bids. It also prevents late entrants from gaming their submissions based on leaders.

### 5. Interview Question Bank Contextually Tied to Model Outputs

**Why it is #5:** This feature cements the "interview prep" positioning without requiring FundSim to build a separate interview prep product. After a student finishes a DCF simulation, serve them the three most commonly asked DCF interview questions — and answer them using the numbers from their specific simulation ("In your model, your discount rate is 9.2% — here is how you would defend that in an interview"). This creates a personalization layer no competitor can replicate without the simulation infrastructure FundSim already has.

**Strategic note:** This also creates a natural upgrade path. Serve 3 contextual interview questions for free; unlock the full question bank for each model type with a Pro subscription.

## 7. What Would Make FundSim "Required" for Finance Students

The threshold from "useful" to "required" requires three simultaneous conditions:

**Condition 1: Social proof that it works for recruiting.** At least 10 publicly documented cases of students who used FundSim deal summaries in their application process and received offers from target firms. Gather these testimonials aggressively. Feature them on the homepage. Make the outcome story — "I ran this LBO on FundSim and referenced it in my Jefferies interview" — the primary conversion message.

**Condition 2: Faculty or club endorsement.** A student who hears about FundSim from a professor or club president views it as a credential, not a tool. Faculty and club adoption converts individual voluntary usage into cohort-level required activity. The professor assignment template (Feature 8) is the mechanism that creates this condition.

**Condition 3: No alternative practice environment exists.** FundSim must build and ship the infrastructure finance module, additional asset classes, and scenario comparison before any competitor recognizes the white space and fills it. The simulation format is reproducible; the first-mover brand association is not. Duolingo did not become "required" for language learners because it was the only app — it became required because it was the first to build daily practice habits into an interactive format that alternatives could not dislodge.

The sequence: **get the shareable URL into one high-profile hiring cycle** (target recruiting season Fall 2026), **partner with one club that posts results publicly**, and **collect 10 outcome testimonials**. Those three events, within a single recruiting season, cross the threshold from "interesting tool" to "thing that finance students at [school] all use."
