## Executive Summary

FundSim operates at the intersection of two powerful, growing trends: the global expansion of alternative asset management (PE/VC/HF AUM growing to $15T+) and the accelerating demand for specialized finance education driven by AI-induced upskilling pressure. The addressable market for finance professional training tools is significantly larger than the consumer edtech lens suggests — when priced at professional rates ($500–$2,400/year), even capturing 1% of the serious finance candidate and professional population generates $30M–$80M in annual revenue.

## Section 3: Growth Trends

### 3A: Is Demand for Finance Education Growing Post-2020?

**Yes, with nuance.**

- CFA registrations peaked at ~230,000 in 2021 and have pulled back to ~157,000 in 2024 (a -32% decline from peak), though the total charterholder base continues to grow at 5.5% annually
- MBA finance concentration remains the top or second-most popular sector for graduates globally
- Private markets (PE/VC/credit/infrastructure) are growing rapidly as an asset class, creating structural demand for practitioners trained in these disciplines
- Key driver: as PE/VC expands beyond traditional finance hubs (NYC, SF, London) into secondary markets and globally, the talent pool needing training expands

**Assessment:** Overall structural demand for finance career training is growing, but the CFA data suggests demand has normalized post-pandemic. The growth opportunity for FundSim is in the shift toward simulation-based, practical training — not exam prep.

### 3B: AI Impact on Finance Education Demand

This is the most powerful tailwind for FundSim.

Key data:

- 64% of finance leaders plan to infuse more technical skills in their functions for FY2025–2026 (FM Magazine, Dec 2025)
- 56% say generative AI is the most prominent skills gap in their organizations in 2025
- JPMorgan mandated GenAI training for every new employee since 2024; Citi began upskilling its entire workforce in prompt writing in September 2025
- Two-thirds of finance graduates consider AI important to their career path; 90%+ believe knowing how to use AI tools would benefit them (CFA Institute 2025 Graduate Survey)
- Financial firms are expected to reduce up to 20% of headcount over the next five years due to AI — which paradoxically increases pressure on remaining practitioners to be more skilled

**The AI paradox creates FundSim's opportunity:** As AI handles rote modeling tasks, humans are expected to demonstrate higher-order judgment, strategic thinking, and deal structuring skills. Interactive simulation is exactly the training modality that builds this. FundSim is positioned to be the "flight simulator for finance," and this framing is validated by the market's direction.

### 3C: Growth of Alternative Assets as an Asset Class

This is the structural driver that makes the PE/IB/VC training market grow independently of economic cycles.

| Metric                                               | Value                   | Source                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Global private markets AUM (2024)                    | ~$15 trillion           | McKinsey / PitchBook                       |
| US private equity AUM (Q3 2024)                      | $3.128 trillion         | S&P Global, Apr 2025                       |
| Global PE dry powder (mid-2024)                      | $2.62 trillion (record) | S&P Global                                 |
| Projected global private markets AUM (2028)          | $18 trillion            | PitchBook base case                        |
| Private markets % of asset mgmt revenue by 2030      | >50%                    | PwC 2025 Global Asset & Wealth Mgmt Report |
| Alternative assets (PE + private debt + real assets) | $21.1 trillion by 2025  | PwC                                        |

**Critical implication:** As private markets go from ~10% to >50% of asset management revenue by 2030, the number of practitioners, analysts, associates, and investors who need deep PE/VC/LBO modeling skills will grow proportionally. This is a secular, multi-decade tailwind.

### 3D: New Fund Launches and Ecosystem Expansion

- Active VC firms globally: ~6,175 (down from 8,315 peak in 2021 due to market correction)
- New interval funds, private fund offerings, and private BDCs launched in 2024: increasing
- PE buyout & growth equity funds in Cambridge Associates database: 1,661 (US only)
- VC funds: 2,625 (US only)
- The denominator effect and LP patience are creating fund manager consolidation near-term, but the long-term trend is clear: more funds, more managers, more analysts needed

Even with consolidation, each fund employs 5–50+ investment professionals who need modeling skills, creating a growing trained-workforce demand.

## Section 5: Adjacent Market Signals

### 5A: CFA Exam Candidates (Proxy for Serious Finance Students)

- 2021 peak: ~230,000 candidates across all 3 levels
- 2024 actual: ~157,000 candidates (Level I: 103,000 in Aug 2024 per CFA Institute)
- Total CFA charterholders: 200,000+ in 160+ countries, growing at 5.5% annually (2012–2024)
- CFA Institute 2025 Graduate Survey: Finance remains the top career choice among recent finance graduates

**Interpretation for FundSim:** CFA candidates represent the "serious student" archetype — motivated, willing to pay, career-focused. However, CFA prep is exam-focused (memorization, formulas), while FundSim's simulation addresses the practical skill gap that CFA does not. These are complementary markets, not competing.

### 5B: MBA Finance Concentration Enrollment

- Finance is the #1 or #2 most popular MBA industry destination at top programs
- Stanford GSB 2025: 37% into finance, median salary $200,000
- Wharton 2025: 36.7% into finance, 205 students
- NYU Stern 2025: 82 grads into finance, median salary $175,000
- ~200,000 full-time MBAs enrolled in US programs annually; ~70,000–80,000 target finance roles

**Interpretation:** The MBA-to-finance pipeline is stable and high-value. These candidates are FundSim's premium user archetype — they are already paying $80,000–$200,000 in tuition and are highly price-insensitive on a $500–$2,000 training tool that directly impacts their compensation.

### 5C: Finance Clubs at Universities

- Every top 50 university in the US has 3–10 finance-related clubs (investment banking, private equity, VC, equity research clubs)
- Aggregate university finance club membership: estimated 100,000–200,000 students annually in the US alone
- Club-based FundSim partnerships (B2B2C) represent a strong distribution channel: 50 clubs × 200 members × $99 group rate = $990,000 ARR from a single university cohort program

## Section 9: Recommended Positioning for Investors

**The headline:** FundSim targets a $700M–$2.5B SAM in professional finance skills training — a market growing at 12–15% annually, with a structural tailwind from both the expansion of alternative asset management ($15T → $18T by 2028) and AI-driven upskilling mandates at every major financial institution.

**The wedge:** No existing platform offers deal-level simulation. BIWS, WSP, and CFI all teach through passive video. FundSim is the first "flight simulator" for finance — analogous to what Bloomberg Market Concepts is for data literacy, but for actual deal execution skills.

**The comparable:** Corporate Finance Institute reached 2 million users and $500M–$1B in estimated revenue with a library of video courses. FundSim's simulation-first approach commands a 2–3x pricing premium (CFI at $497/yr vs. FundSim target $800–$2,400/yr) and a more defensible moat.
